## Aim of the game

**Be the first to empty your hand.** The player who manages it scores 0 points
for the round; the others score 2 points per card they have left.

The game stops as soon as a player reaches **50 points**. The player with the
**lowest** total wins.

## What's in the box

**72 cards**:

- **64 number cards** from 1 to 8, with eight copies of each value;
- **8 double cards**, which carry two values: 1/2, 3/4, 5/6 and 7/8.

## Setup

| Players | Cards per player |
|---|---|
| 3 | 10 |
| 4 or 5 | 8 |

The remaining cards form the draw pile. The table is empty at the start of the
round.

## The rule that changes everything: you never sort your hand

**The order of your cards never changes.** You keep them exactly as they were
dealt, without rearranging them.

You decide only one thing: **where to insert** the cards that come into your
hand, whether they come from the draw pile or from the table.

## Combinations

A combination is **one or more cards of the same value that are neighbours in
your hand**.

For example, with this hand:

> 3 – 5 – 5 – 2 – 5 – 8

- the first two **5s** are neighbours: that is a pair;
- the third **5** is separated from them by the 2: it does not count with
  them;
- if you play the **2** on its own, the three 5s become neighbours: you now
  hold three of a kind for a later turn.

Playing a lone card to bring two groups together is the most important move in
the game.

### Double cards

A double card is worth one of its two values, **chosen when you play it**. A
5/6 card sitting between a 5 and a 6 can complete either one.

## How a turn works

### If the table is empty

You **lead** with any combination you like.

### If there is a combination on the table

You have two options.

**1. Play a combination that beats the one on the table.** It must have:

- **more cards**, whatever their value;
- or **the same number of cards, of a higher value**.

You then collect the cards you have just beaten: you insert them anywhere you
like in your hand, or you discard them.

**2. Draw a card.** You insert it into your hand wherever you like, or you
discard it. Then your turn passes to the next player.

### Examples: what beats what?

| On the table | You play | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A pair of 7s | A pair of 8s | **Beaten**: same number of cards, higher value |
| A pair of 7s | Three 2s | **Beaten**: more cards |
| A pair of 7s | A pair of 6s | Not allowed: lower value |
| Three 4s | A pair of 8s | Not allowed: fewer cards |
| A single 8 | A pair of 1s | **Beaten**: more cards |

So the number of cards always comes before their value.

## The Jungo

When you draw, look at the card you picked up: if it lets you **beat the
table**, alone or with identical cards next to it in your hand, you may **play
it right away**. That's a **Jungo**.

Example: a pair of 7s is on the table and you hold two 5s side by side, nothing
that beats it. You draw a 5 and slip it in next to the other two: three 5s beat
a pair of 7s. Jungo.

## When nobody beats the table

If your combination goes all the way round the table **without being beaten**,
it is discarded. The table is empty, and you **play freely again**.

## Collect the beaten cards or discard them?

Taking back the cards you have just beaten makes your hand heavier, and every
card costs 2 points at the end of the round. But two well-placed cards can turn
a pair into four of a kind.

The rule of thumb: only take them if they **complete straight away** a group
you already hold. Otherwise, discard them.

## Scoring

As soon as a player has emptied their hand, the round stops:

| Player | Points for the round |
|---|---|
| The one who emptied their hand | **0** |
| A player with 3 cards left | **6** |
| A player with 7 cards left | **14** |

Only the **number** of cards counts: an 8 costs no more than a 1.

## End of the game

Rounds follow one another until a player reaches **50 points**. The player
with the lowest total wins.

## The four most common mistakes

1. **Sorting your hand** by value after the deal. It is not allowed.
2. **Playing identical cards that are not neighbours**. They must be touching
   in your hand.
3. **Thinking a high value is enough**. A pair of 8s does not beat three 1s.
4. **Counting the value of the cards** when scoring. It is 2 points per card,
   whatever the card.

## Three tips for beginners

- **Insert every card next to its twins**: a drawn card put in the wrong place
  is a wasted card.
- **Play your lone cards to bring your groups together**, rather than breaking
  up a pair.
- **Save your big combinations to take the lead back**: the player whose
  combination is not beaten then leads with whatever they want.
