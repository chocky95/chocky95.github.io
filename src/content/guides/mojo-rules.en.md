## Aim of the game

Score **as few points as possible**. A game is played over several rounds: it
stops as soon as a player reaches **50 points**, and the player with the
**lowest score** wins.

So the whole game is about getting rid of your cards, and above all the highest
ones, before the round ends.

## What's in the box

**78 cards** numbered 0 to 12, in five colours. Each colour covers a band of
values:

| Colour | Values | Cards |
|---|---|---|
| Blue | 0 and 1 | 8 (4 of each) |
| Green | 2, 3 and 4 | 15 (5 of each) |
| Yellow | 5, 6 and 7 | 18 (6 of each) |
| Orange | 8, 9 and 10 | 21 (7 of each) |
| Red | 11 and 12 | 16 (8 of each) |

The higher a card, the more copies there are: there are twice as many 12s as
0s.

On top of those comes the **Mojo card**, which has no value and shows the
direction of play.

## Setup

1. Deal **8 cards** to each player.
2. Place the Mojo card in the centre: play goes clockwise.
3. Form the draw pile with the rest, and turn over its top card to start the
   **discard pile**.

## How a turn works

On your turn, you play **one card from your hand** onto the discard pile. Its
value, compared with the top card, decides what happens next:

| Card played | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Lower** value | Your turn ends, without drawing |
| **Equal** value | You **must** play another card |
| **Higher** value | You draw a card, then your turn ends |

Colour plays no part during the turn: only the value matters. Colour is only
used for scoring.

### Example

The discard pile shows a 7.

- You play a **4**: lower, so your turn ends. You have one card fewer.
- You play a **7**: equal, so you play again straight away. That is two cards
  gone in one turn if the next one is lower or equal.
- You play an **11**: higher, so you draw. Your hand keeps the same number of
  cards, but you have swapped an 11 for an unknown card.

Playing a higher card is therefore not a mistake: it is the only way to part
with a big card when the discard pile is low.

## Mojo Time

As soon as a player has only **three cards or fewer** in hand at the end of
their turn, they enter **Mojo Time**:

1. they place their remaining cards **face down** in front of them;
2. on each of their following turns, they **reveal one of those cards** instead
   of playing;
3. the round ends as soon as a player has revealed **their last card**.

The other players carry on playing normally in the meantime: they have a few
turns left to bring their hand down. Several players can be in Mojo Time at
once.

The **first** player to enter Mojo Time takes the Mojo card. That player has
the most at stake when the scores are counted.

## Scoring

At the end of the round, each player counts the cards they have left:

> **For each colour, only the highest card counts.** You add up those values,
> one per colour.

### Worked examples

| Cards left | Points | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blue 1, green 3, green 4 | **5** | 1 for blue, 4 for green |
| Orange 9, orange 10 | **10** | A single colour: the highest card |
| Red 12, red 12 | **12** | Two identical cards count only once |
| Blue 1, green 4, yellow 7, orange 10, red 12 | **34** | One card per colour: the worst possible total |

So keeping three orange cards costs no more than keeping one. What costs you
is holding **lots of different colours**.

### The countered player

The first player to enter Mojo Time is scored differently:

- if their total is **lower than or equal to** every other player's, they score
  **0 points**;
- if their total is **higher** than at least one opponent's, they are
  **countered**: they score their total **plus a 10-point** penalty.

A countered player also turns the table around: in the next round, **the
direction of play is reversed**.

Example: you enter Mojo Time with a green 2, a green 3 and an orange 8, which
makes 12 points. If an opponent finishes the round with 9 points, you are
countered and score 22 points instead of 0. Entering Mojo Time with low cards
is therefore much safer than entering it quickly.

## When the draw pile is empty

When the draw pile runs out, the discard pile is shuffled to form a new draw
pile. The top card stays where it is.

## End of the game

The game stops at the end of the round in which a player reaches **50
points**. The player with the lowest total wins.

## The two-discard-pile variant

In this variant, two discard piles coexist, each with its own top card.

- You choose which pile to play on. A 7 is "higher" on a 6, but "lower" on
  a 9.
- Once your first card is played, your turn stays on that pile.
- When you have to draw, you may take the top card of **the other discard
  pile** rather than the top card of the draw pile: you know what you are
  getting.

The right habit is to keep one pile low, so you can play again easily, and one
pile high, to leave your big cards on.

## The four most common mistakes

1. **Adding up all your cards** when scoring. Only the highest card of each
   colour counts.
2. **Thinking that playing again is optional** after an equal card. It is
   compulsory.
3. **Forgetting the countered player's penalty**: finishing first only scores 0
   if nobody did better.
4. **Entering Mojo Time with big cards**. Three cards in three different
   colours can cost up to 29 points, plus 10.

## Three tips for beginners

- **Get rid of red and orange first**: these are the colours that weigh on
  your score.
- **Group your colours**: you are better off finishing with three green cards
  than with a blue, a green and a yellow.
- **Keep your equal cards to chain plays**: a duplicate played on its twin
  sends two cards away in a single turn.
