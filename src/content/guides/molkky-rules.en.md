## The aim of the game

Be the first player — or team — to reach **exactly 50 points** by knocking down
numbered pins.

That word *exactly* is the whole game. Going past 50 does not win: it drops your
score to 25. A game of Mölkky is decided at the end as much as at the start.

A quick variant sets the target at **30 points**.

## What you need

- **12 wooden pins**, numbered 1 to 12
- **1 throwing baton**, the *mölkky* — a wooden stick with no number
- something to keep score on

## Setting up

The 12 pins stand in a **tight cluster**, touching each other, in this
arrangement:

| Row | Pins |
|---|---|
| Back | 7 – 9 – 8 |
| Third | 5 – 11 – 12 – 6 |
| Second | 3 – 10 – 4 |
| Front | 1 – 2 |

The low numbers sit at the front, the high ones in the middle and at the back.
That is what makes big scores hard to get on an opening throw.

The **throwing line** is drawn **3 to 4 metres** from the pins, and does not
move afterwards.

## How a turn works

1. The player throws the mölkky at the pins from behind the throwing line.
2. Fallen pins are **stood back up exactly where they came to rest**.

That second point is what gives the game its shape: the cluster scatters as play
goes on, and hitting one specific pin becomes progressively harder.

## Scoring

Two cases, and only one thing separates them:

> **One pin falls** → you score **that pin's number**.
>
> **Several pins fall** → you score **how many pins** went down.

### Worked examples

| What falls | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 12, on its own | **12** | One pin: its face value |
| Pins 12, 11 and 3 | **3** | Three pins: their count, not 26 |
| Pin 1, on its own | **1** | One pin: its face value |
| Any seven pins | **7** | Seven pins knocked down |

Here is the mistake almost everyone makes: **adding the numbers up**. Don't. As
soon as two pins fall, only the count matters. Knocking down pins 11 and 12
scores **2 points**, not 23.

### When does a pin count?

Only when it is **completely on the ground**. A pin that is tilted, or resting
against another pin, has not fallen.

## Going over 50

If a player goes past the target, **their score drops back to 25**.

This is what makes the endgame tactical. On 47 points, the last thing you want
is to scatter a cluster — you need pin number 3, specifically. Plenty of players
sitting on 48 or 49 will take a risky throw at a single isolated low pin rather
than aim at the group.

## The three-miss rule

**Three consecutive throws without touching a single pin** knock the player out.

Some groups prefer a gentler variant: the score resets to zero but the player
keeps going. Agree on this before you start — it is a classic source of
argument.

A throw that touches a pin without fully knocking it over scores 0, but **does
not count as a miss**.

## Ending the game, and sets

The game stops the moment a player hits the target exactly.

To make it last, matches are usually played as **sets**: first to two or three
wins takes the match. That is the standard tournament format.

## Common variants

### Teams

Team members throw in turn, alternating with the other team. The score is
shared. Every other rule is unchanged.

### Two players

The rules do not change. Two-player games are normally played as a best-of-three
so there is more to play for.

### Quick game

Target 30 points instead of 50. Good for a short round, or for teaching someone
the game.

## The four scoring mistakes people actually make

1. **Adding the numbers up** when several pins fall. It is the count that
   scores.
2. **Forgetting the drop to 25** after going over 50.
3. **Counting a leaning pin**, or one propped on another. It has to be flat on
   the ground.
4. **Putting the pins back in their starting positions**. They stand up where
   they landed.

## Where does Mölkky come from?

Mölkky is a Finnish game, derived from *kyykkä*, a far older Karelian pin game.
It is played mostly in Finland, Sweden and Estonia, in Japan — which has a real
competitive scene — and increasingly in France and Germany.
